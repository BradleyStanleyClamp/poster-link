Optimising friendship for maximum ticket yield.

The idea

The more of us involved, the better our odds: any successful purchase covers 6 reg numbers, not just the buyer's own group.

Show the maths

If each person has an independent chance p of getting through the queue in time, and n people from your network are all queuing, the odds that at least one of them makes it are:

P(at least 1) = 1 − (1 − p)n

That climbs fast as n grows — and since any single success can cover 6 people's worth of tickets, every extra person queuing (even from someone else's group) helps everyone, not just themselves. With ~130,000 tickets on sale and ~2.5 million people registered, that's roughly 21,700 successful purchases up for grabs (130,000 ÷ 6) — so any one random person's odds of being one of them are just p ≈ 0.9%. Pick your crew

Start a group of 6, or join one a friend's already started. Because loads of different friend circles are piling in, we alline groups up next to the people they already know best (in a looped chain), so if you do well, you will be helping your closest freinds the most.

On the day

Simple rules. Get a ticket and your group's still empty? It's yours. Your group's already sorted? Pass it to the next group in the line that is ticketless. Repeat all the way round until we all win.
